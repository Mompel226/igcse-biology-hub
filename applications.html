Draw the room once, then drag faces into chairs

Import a class, lay out the benches the way your room actually is, and drag photographs into seats. Shuffle them, sort them A–Z, or split up whoever needs splitting up. It prints on one sheet, and Student's view turns the plan round so it reads from where they are sitting.

  • Photographs, so you learn names in the first week rather than the third
  • Several plans per class — the practical is not the test
  • Quiet labels only you can read, for who needs what
  • One sheet of A4, or the whole room on the screen

A window that finds you, in the morning

Wishing a fourteen-year-old a happy birthday costs nothing and lands more than most of what we do that week. It fails because nobody tells you in time. This checks when you log in and within five minutes of you opening the lid, then leaves you alone for the rest of the day.

  • Friday covers the weekend, so you can say it to their face
  • Catches up on the days the Mac was shut
  • Discreet mode: spots a projector and hides the name until you click
  • A calendar to browse, and a panel for preferred names

Put your app on the shelf

This shelf is not only mine. If you have written something that makes teaching easier — a marking helper, a rota, a lab booking sheet, anything — other people are almost certainly doing the same job by hand. Three things and it can sit here beside the rest.

  • Put the code somewhere public, so people can read it before they run it
  • Write a read-me that says what it needs — the system, and any school data it expects
  • Add one screenshot, taken with invented pupils and never a real child
