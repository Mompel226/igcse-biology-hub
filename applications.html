Apps for teachers

Two small Mac apps written for my own teaching and free for any school to use. Neither needs an account, a server, or anyone's permission.

Made by Dr Daniel Mompel Riera

Seating Plan

Draw the room once, then drag faces into chairs

Import a class from two iSAMS exports, lay out the benches the way your room actually is, and drag photographs into seats. Shuffle them, sort them A–Z, or split up whoever needs splitting up. It prints on one sheet, and Student's view turns the plan round so it reads from where they are sitting.

  • Photographs, so you learn names in the first week rather than the third
  • Several plans per class — the practical is not the test
  • Quiet labels only you can read, for who needs what
  • One sheet of A4, or the whole room on the screen

Birthday Reminder

A window that finds you, in the morning

Wishing a fourteen-year-old a happy birthday costs nothing and lands more than most of what we do that week. It fails because nobody tells you in time. This checks when you log in and within five minutes of you opening the lid, then leaves you alone for the rest of the day.

  • Friday covers the weekend, so you can say it to their face
  • Catches up on the days the Mac was shut
  • Discreet mode: spots a projector and hides the name until you click
  • A calendar to browse, and a panel for preferred names

Two things worth knowing

Nothing leaves your Mac. Each app is a small server that only your own browser can reach, on a random port, behind a token made when it starts. There is no account, no cloud and no outbound request of any kind. Your classes sit in a folder you can open, read and delete yourself.

macOS will block them the first time, because they are not signed by a paid Apple developer account. That is expected, and each app's read-me walks through the three clicks past it. You do it once.
